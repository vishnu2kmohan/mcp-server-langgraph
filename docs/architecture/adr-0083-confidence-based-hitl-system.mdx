# 83. Confidence-Based Human-in-the-Loop Decision Making

Date: 2025-12-26

## Status

Accepted

## Category

Agent Architecture

## Context

As AI agents become more capable and handle increasingly critical operations, the traditional binary approach to Human-in-the-Loop (HITL) decision making—where every action requires explicit human approval—creates several operational challenges:

- **Review Fatigue**: Operators become overwhelmed by constant approval requests, leading to rubber-stamping behavior
- **Reduced Safety**: Alert fatigue causes operators to approve risky actions without careful review
- **Decreased Throughput**: Every action requiring approval creates bottlenecks and delays
- **Poor User Experience**: Users must wait for approval even for routine, low-risk operations
- **Operational Overhead**: High-volume deployments require dedicated operators for trivial approvals

However, removing HITL controls entirely introduces unacceptable risks:
- **Unintended Consequences**: AI may execute actions with unforeseen side effects
- **Policy Violations**: Actions may violate organizational policies or regulatory requirements
- **Security Incidents**: Malicious inputs could trigger harmful actions
- **Accountability Gaps**: No human oversight for critical operations

The challenge is to **maintain safety while reducing operator burden** by identifying which actions truly require human judgment and which can be safely automated.

## Decision

We will implement a **confidence-based HITL system** that uses AI confidence scoring to automatically approve high-confidence actions while routing low-confidence decisions to human operators.

### Core Principles

1. **Confidence Thresholds**: Actions are evaluated on a 0.0-1.0 confidence scale
2. **Auto-Approval Above Threshold**: High-confidence actions (≥0.85 by default) execute automatically
3. **Human Review Below Threshold**: Low-confidence actions pause for explicit approval
4. **AI Explanations**: Low-confidence requests include AI-generated explanations of risks and uncertainties
5. **Real-Time Delivery**: WebSocket-based delivery ensures operators receive requests immediately
6. **Audit Trail**: All decisions (auto-approved and manual) are logged for compliance

### Confidence Scoring Model

```python
class ConfidenceScore:
    """Confidence assessment for HITL decision"""
    score: float  # 0.0 (no confidence) to 1.0 (complete confidence)
    factors: Dict[str, float]  # Contributing factors (policy_match, risk_level, etc.)
    explanation: Optional[str]  # Why confidence is low (if score < threshold)
    recommendation: Literal["approve", "reject", "needs_review"]
```

Confidence factors include:
- **Policy Alignment**: How well the action matches known policies (0.0-1.0)
- **Risk Assessment**: Estimated impact of action failure (0.0-1.0)
- **Context Clarity**: Completeness of contextual information (0.0-1.0)
- **Historical Success**: Success rate of similar past actions (0.0-1.0)
- **Authorization Strength**: Strength of user permissions for this action (0.0-1.0)

## Consequences

### Positive Consequences

- **Reduced Operator Fatigue**: Routine high-confidence actions execute automatically
- **Faster Processing**: Auto-approval reduces latency from minutes to milliseconds
- **Focused Attention**: Operators review only genuinely uncertain actions
- **Better Decision Quality**: AI explanations provide context for informed decisions
- **Scalability**: System handles 10x more actions without proportional operator increase
- **Audit Compliance**: Full logging of all decisions (auto + manual) for compliance

### Negative Consequences

- **Calibration Challenge**: Confidence thresholds must be carefully tuned per deployment
- **False Confidence Risk**: AI may be overconfident on edge cases (mitigated by monitoring)
- **Explanation Quality**: AI explanations may be vague or misleading (requires validation)
- **Complexity**: Additional system components (scoring, WebSocket, monitoring)
- **Trust Calibration**: Operators must learn to trust auto-approval (change management)

### Neutral Consequences

- **Tuning Required**: Different organizations will need different thresholds
- **Monitoring Overhead**: Must track auto-approval accuracy and adjust thresholds
- **Fallback Required**: System must handle WebSocket failures gracefully

## Alternatives Considered

### 1. All-Manual HITL (Current State)

**Description**: Every action requires explicit human approval

**Pros**:
- Maximum human oversight
- Simple implementation
- Clear accountability

**Cons**:
- Operator fatigue and alert fatigue
- Bottleneck for high-volume operations
- Poor user experience (delays)
- Does not scale

**Why Rejected**: Operational overhead and review fatigue create unacceptable safety and throughput issues

### 2. All-Automated (No HITL)

**Description**: AI executes all actions without human approval

**Pros**:
- Maximum throughput
- Minimal latency
- No operator overhead

**Cons**:
- Unacceptable safety risk
- No human judgment for edge cases
- Compliance/regulatory concerns
- Accountability gaps

**Why Rejected**: Safety and compliance requirements mandate human oversight for uncertain actions

### 3. Rule-Based Auto-Approval

**Description**: Predefined rules determine which actions auto-approve (e.g., "read-only operations")

**Pros**:
- Deterministic and auditable
- Easy to understand
- No AI calibration needed

**Cons**:
- Brittle (hard-coded rules break on edge cases)
- Difficult to maintain (rule explosion)
- Cannot adapt to new scenarios
- Misses nuanced risk factors

**Why Rejected**: Static rules cannot capture complex risk assessments that consider multiple contextual factors

### 4. Time-Based Auto-Approval

**Description**: Auto-approve actions during business hours, require approval after hours

**Pros**:
- Simple temporal logic
- Reduces after-hours paging
- Predictable behavior

**Cons**:
- Time is poor proxy for risk
- High-risk actions still happen during business hours
- Does not reduce daytime operator burden
- Geographic time zone complexity

**Why Rejected**: Time does not correlate with action risk; does not address operator fatigue during business hours

## Implementation Details

### Confidence Scoring Engine

```python
# src/mcp_server_langgraph/agent/hitl/confidence.py
class ConfidenceScorer:
    """Compute confidence score for HITL decisions"""

    def __init__(self, threshold: float = 0.85):
        self.threshold = threshold

    async def score_action(
        self,
        action: AgentAction,
        context: ExecutionContext,
        policy: Optional[Policy] = None,
    ) -> ConfidenceScore:
        """
        Compute confidence score for action approval

        Args:
            action: Proposed agent action
            context: Execution context (user, permissions, history)
            policy: Optional policy constraints

        Returns:
            ConfidenceScore with overall score and contributing factors
        """
        factors = {
            "policy_alignment": await self._score_policy_alignment(action, policy),
            "risk_level": await self._score_risk_level(action),
            "context_clarity": await self._score_context_clarity(context),
            "historical_success": await self._score_historical_success(action),
            "authorization_strength": await self._score_authorization(context),
        }

        # Weighted average (configurable weights)
        overall_score = sum(
            factors[k] * self.weights[k]
            for k in factors
        )

        # Generate explanation if below threshold
        explanation = None
        recommendation = "approve"
        if overall_score < self.threshold:
            explanation = await self._generate_explanation(action, factors)
            recommendation = "needs_review"

        return ConfidenceScore(
            score=overall_score,
            factors=factors,
            explanation=explanation,
            recommendation=recommendation,
        )
```

### AI Explanation Generation

```python
async def _generate_explanation(
    self,
    action: AgentAction,
    factors: Dict[str, float],
) -> str:
    """
    Generate human-readable explanation of low confidence

    Uses LLM to explain why action requires human review
    """
    prompt = f"""
    The following action has been flagged for human review due to low confidence.

    Action: {action.description}
    Confidence Factors:
    - Policy Alignment: {factors['policy_alignment']:.2f}
    - Risk Level: {factors['risk_level']:.2f}
    - Context Clarity: {factors['context_clarity']:.2f}
    - Historical Success: {factors['historical_success']:.2f}
    - Authorization Strength: {factors['authorization_strength']:.2f}

    Explain in 2-3 sentences why this action requires human review.
    Focus on the specific risks or uncertainties that make automation unsafe.
    """

    llm = create_llm_from_config(settings)
    response = await llm.ainvoke([{"role": "user", "content": prompt}])

    return response.content
```

### WebSocket Delivery

```python
# src/mcp_server_langgraph/agent/hitl/websocket.py
class HITLWebSocketManager:
    """Manage real-time HITL approval requests via WebSocket"""

    async def send_approval_request(
        self,
        action: AgentAction,
        confidence: ConfidenceScore,
        timeout: int = 300,
    ) -> HITLDecision:
        """
        Send approval request to operator via WebSocket

        Args:
            action: Action requiring approval
            confidence: Confidence score with explanation
            timeout: Max wait time in seconds

        Returns:
            HITLDecision (approved/rejected)
        """
        request_id = str(uuid.uuid4())

        # Publish to operator WebSocket channel
        await self.redis.publish(
            "hitl:approval_requests",
            json.dumps({
                "request_id": request_id,
                "action": action.model_dump(),
                "confidence": confidence.model_dump(),
                "expires_at": (datetime.now() + timedelta(seconds=timeout)).isoformat(),
            })
        )

        # Wait for response (with timeout)
        try:
            async with asyncio.timeout(timeout):
                decision = await self._wait_for_decision(request_id)
                return decision
        except TimeoutError:
            # Escalate to supervisor or reject by default
            return HITLDecision(
                request_id=request_id,
                decision="rejected",
                reason="Approval timeout exceeded",
            )
```

### Auto-Approval Logic

```python
# src/mcp_server_langgraph/agent/hitl/approver.py
class HITLApprover:
    """Coordinate HITL approval decisions"""

    async def request_approval(
        self,
        action: AgentAction,
        context: ExecutionContext,
    ) -> HITLDecision:
        """
        Request approval for action (auto-approve or human review)

        Returns:
            HITLDecision indicating approval/rejection
        """
        # Compute confidence score
        confidence = await self.scorer.score_action(action, context)

        # Auto-approve if high confidence
        if confidence.score >= self.threshold and self.auto_approve_enabled:
            decision = HITLDecision(
                request_id=str(uuid.uuid4()),
                decision="auto_approved",
                confidence=confidence.score,
                timestamp=datetime.now(),
            )
            await self._log_decision(decision)
            return decision

        # Request human approval via WebSocket
        decision = await self.websocket_manager.send_approval_request(
            action=action,
            confidence=confidence,
            timeout=self.approval_timeout,
        )

        await self._log_decision(decision)
        return decision
```

## Migration Path

**Phase 1: Shadow Mode** (2 weeks)
- Enable confidence scoring but log only (no auto-approval)
- Collect baseline data on score distribution
- Validate explanation quality with operators

**Phase 2: Pilot Deployment** (2 weeks)
- Enable auto-approval for single low-risk action type
- Monitor auto-approval outcomes closely
- Gather operator feedback

**Phase 3: Gradual Rollout** (4 weeks)
- Expand to additional action types based on pilot results
- Tune thresholds per action type
- Scale to production traffic

**Rollback**: Disable auto-approval via `HITL_AUTO_APPROVE_ENABLED=false` (immediate effect)

## Security Considerations

**Adversarial Inputs**: Malicious users may craft inputs designed to boost confidence scores
- **Mitigation**: Confidence factors include authorization strength and policy alignment
- **Monitoring**: Anomaly detection on confidence score patterns

**False Confidence**: AI may be overconfident on edge cases not seen in training data
- **Mitigation**: Conservative threshold (0.85 default, tunable to 0.90+)
- **Monitoring**: Track auto-approval failure rates and adjust thresholds

**Explanation Manipulation**: AI-generated explanations may be misleading
- **Mitigation**: Template-based explanations with structured data
- **Validation**: Human review of explanation quality during pilot phase

## Future Enhancements

- **Per-Action-Type Thresholds**: Different thresholds for read vs. write operations
- **User-Specific Thresholds**: Higher auto-approval for trusted users
- **Learning from Feedback**: Train on operator decisions to improve scoring
- **Multi-Level Approval**: Route high-risk actions to senior operators
- **Confidence Trend Analysis**: Detect drift in confidence scores over time
